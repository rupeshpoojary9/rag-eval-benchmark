# Results & Analysis

Corpus: **17 documents, 95 chunks** (paragraph chunking). Gold set: **40 hand-labelled
questions**, span-level labels. Retrieval is deterministic; re-running reproduces this
table. Generated by `rag-eval run --faithfulness`.

## Main results

| strategy | recall@1 | recall@3 | recall@5 | recall@10 | MRR@10 | nDCG@10 | answer_corr | faithfulness |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| bm25 | 0.375 | 0.637 | 0.738 | 0.838 | 0.538 | 0.614 | 0.418 | 1.000 |
| dense | 0.537 | 0.700 | 0.863 | 0.912 | 0.668 | 0.728 | 0.442 | 1.000 |
| hybrid_rrf | 0.537 | 0.812 | **0.925** | 0.925 | 0.698 | 0.761 | 0.457 | 1.000 |
| hybrid_rrf+rerank | **0.600** | **0.838** | 0.912 | 0.912 | **0.730** | **0.781** | 0.451 | 1.000 |

**Headline:** hybrid (RRF) lifts **recall@5 from 0.738 (BM25) to 0.925 — +25% relative**.
Adding a cross-encoder reranker lifts **MRR@10 from 0.698 to 0.730** and **recall@1 from
0.537 to 0.600**, i.e. it sharpens the *top* of the ranking.

## What the numbers say

**1. Each stage adds real, measured value.** BM25 → dense → hybrid → hybrid+rerank
improves monotonically on the metrics each stage targets. This is the whole point of the
benchmark: "we do hybrid + rerank" is now a number, not a claim.

**2. The gain comes almost entirely from vocabulary mismatch — recall@5 by phrasing:**

| strategy | paraphrase | synonym | verbatim |
|---|---|---|---|
| bm25 | 0.750 | 0.556 | **1.000** |
| dense | 0.885 | 0.722 | 1.000 |
| hybrid_rrf | 0.942 | **0.833** | 1.000 |
| hybrid_rrf+rerank | 0.923 | 0.833 | 1.000 |

BM25 is **perfect (1.000) on verbatim questions** and collapses to **0.556 on synonym
questions** — questions phrased in user language that share no words with the source.
Dense retrieval recovers most of that gap (0.556 → 0.722) and hybrid closes it further
(→ 0.833). This is exactly the failure mode dense retrieval exists to fix, and it's why
the eval set was deliberately written with synonym/paraphrase phrasings — a benchmark
where every question quotes its source would have shown BM25 winning and hidden the
effect entirely.

**3. Reranking trades a little recall breadth for top precision — an honest tradeoff.**
hybrid+rerank *beats* hybrid on recall@1 (0.600 vs 0.537), MRR (0.730 vs 0.698), and
nDCG (0.781 vs 0.761), but is fractionally *lower* on recall@5 (0.912 vs 0.925). A
reranker only reorders the candidates it's given; it pulls the single best passage
upward (helping MRR/recall@1) but can also demote a relevant passage out of the top 5.
Which you prefer depends on the consumer: a single-citation answer wants MRR, a
top-5-into-the-prompt generator wants recall@5.

**4. recall@k and MRR genuinely disagree — which is why both are reported.** hybrid_rrf
has the best recall@5 but hybrid_rrf+rerank has the best MRR. Reporting only one would
pick a different "winner." A change that widens the net raises recall while pushing the
single best passage down, lowering MRR.

## Chunk-size ablation (recall@5, fixed-window chunking)

| chunk size (words) | bm25 | dense | hybrid_rrf | hybrid_rrf+rerank |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 60 | 0.650 | 0.738 | 0.825 | 0.887 |
| 120 | 0.700 | 0.825 | 0.900 | **0.938** |
| 240 | 0.850 | 0.887 | 0.912 | 0.912 |

Two readings, both worth stating out loud:

- **Larger chunks raise raw recall@5 across every strategy**, and BM25 gains the most
  (0.650 → 0.850). Part of this is a *metric artifact*: a bigger chunk overlaps more of
  the labelled span, so "did a relevant chunk appear" gets mechanically easier. This is
  the trap of optimising recall@k alone — it rewards bloated chunks that also dilute the
  embedding and stuff the generator's context. It's precisely why answer quality and MRR
  must be watched alongside recall.
- **The best single config here is hybrid+rerank at 120-word chunks (0.938).** At 240
  words the strategies converge as recall saturates, and the reranker's edge disappears
  because there's little left to reorder.

## Retrieval quality → answer quality

`answer_corr` (answer correctness vs the gold answer) rises with retrieval quality —
**0.418 (BM25) → 0.457 (hybrid)** — because a better retriever puts the answer-bearing
passage in the context more often, so the generated answer can actually contain it.

Meanwhile `faithfulness` (is the answer grounded *in the retrieved context*) is a flat
**1.000** for every strategy. That is not a bug — it's the honest consequence of the
offline generator being **extractive**: it can only quote passages it was given, so it
is faithful to the context by construction, whatever the context contains. The two
metrics measure different things: *faithfulness* is grounding-in-context (controllable
by the pipeline), *correctness* is match-to-truth (gated by retrieval). Faithfulness
only becomes an interesting, varying signal with a real generator that *can*
hallucinate — which is exactly what the `RAG_EVAL_LLM=anthropic` /
`RAG_EVAL_JUDGE=anthropic` swap enables.

## What I'd use in production

- **Default to hybrid (RRF) + a cross-encoder reranker.** RRF needs no score
  normalisation and is robust; the reranker buys the top-rank precision that matters when
  you feed a small k into the generator.
- **Tune k and chunk size on a labelled set, not by feel.** The ablation shows both move
  the metrics materially, and the right point is corpus-specific.
- **Never optimise recall@k alone.** It's gameable with large chunks. Watch MRR/nDCG and
  an answer-quality metric together.
- **The eval set is the asset.** Every decision above is only trustworthy because there's
  a labelled gold set to measure against — building it was the hard part, and it pays for
  itself the first time it catches a change that looked good but lowered recall.

## Reproduce

```bash
rag-eval run --faithfulness --plots        # main table + plots → results/
rag-eval ablation --sizes 60,120,240       # ablation table → results/
```
